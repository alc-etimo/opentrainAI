Two Fluent Answers. One Better Response.

A demonstration of a repeatable process for evaluating AI-generated responses, rather than relying on fluency alone.

User Prompt

"Explain the difference between 'por que', 'porque', 'por quê', and 'porquê' in Brazilian Portuguese, with examples."

Response A

In Portuguese, these words are often confusing. "Por que" is used in questions, like "Por que você fez isso?". "Porque" is used for answers, like "Porque eu quis". "Por quê" is used at the end of sentences, like "Você fez isso por quê?". "Porquê" is a noun, like "O porquê da questão".

Response B

"Por que" (two words, no accent) is used at the beginning or middle of a question, meaning "why" or "for what reason." Example: "Por que você está triste?"

"Porque" (one word, no accent) is a conjunction meaning "because." Example: "Estou triste porque choveu."

"Por quê" (two words, with circumflex) is used at the end of a sentence or before a pause, also meaning "why." Example: "Você está triste por quê?"

"Porquê" (one word, with circumflex) is a noun meaning "the reason." Example: "Não entendi o porquê da sua tristeza."

Criterion Response A Response B
Accuracy 3/4 Mostly correct but lacks nuance. 4/4 Accurate and complete.
Reasoning 2/4 Provides examples but doesn't fully explain the logic. 4/4 Clearly explains grammatical rules with examples.
Instruction Adherence 2/4 Answers the prompt but lacks structure. 4/4 Directly addresses each term systematically.
Clarity & Consistency 2/4 Compact but slightly confusing. 4/4 Clear, well-structured, and consistent.

Written Rationale

Response B is preferable. While both responses are grammatically fluent in Portuguese, Response B provides a more complete and systematic explanation of the four terms. It clearly defines each usage, provides accurate examples, and maintains a consistent structure that makes the explanation easier to follow. Response A is correct in its examples but lacks the explanatory depth and structure needed for a user who is genuinely confused by these terms.

Confidence: High. The evaluation is based on explicit criteria (accuracy, reasoning, instruction adherence, clarity) and cites specific evidence from the responses.

When Correct Portuguese Is Not the Most Appropriate Portuguese

Demonstrating that language quality is contextual: a version may be grammatically acceptable yet unsuitable for the intended audience, tone, or task.

Source Context

"Our app is designed to help you manage your tasks efficiently. Please click the button below to get started."

Target Audience: Brazilian young professionals using a productivity app.

Desired Tone: Direct, modern, and friendly.

Option 1 (Literal)

"Nosso aplicativo foi projetado para ajudá-lo a gerenciar suas tarefas com eficiência. Por favor, clique no botão abaixo para começar."

Option 2 (Contextual)

"Nosso app foi feito para te ajudar a organizar suas tarefas sem complicação. Bora começar? É só clicar no botão abaixo."

Rationale for Preference

Option 2 is preferable. While Option 1 is grammatically correct and accurate, it sounds overly formal and bureaucratic for the target audience. Option 2 uses more natural, conversational Brazilian Portuguese ("app" instead of "aplicativo", "te ajudar" instead of "ajudá-lo", "bora começar" as a friendly call to action). This aligns better with the modern, user-friendly tone of a productivity app aimed at young professionals.

Note: Option 1 might be preferable in a formal, legal, or corporate context where neutrality and precision are paramount.
